Your agent identity

Creating the agent registers it in Monad's ERC-8004 Identity Registry (owned by your identity key) and sets your scanner key as its agent wallet. From then on, every time your verdict agrees or disagrees with the consensus, the Nadeye contract writes it to the ERC-8004 Reputation Registry.

From an image to a record

  1. The extension scores the image on your device. A ViT-S/16 model runs in the browser on WebGPU, with WASM as a fallback. Pixels are never uploaded.
  2. It hashes the pixels. A 64-bit DCT perceptual hash summarizes the image. It survives resizing and recompression and cannot be turned back into the picture.
  3. Confident verdicts go to Monad in batches. Once a minute, or as soon as 25 are waiting, up to 50 go out in one attestBatch transaction signed by the scanner key in the extension.
  4. The next person sees the count. Anyone who meets that image again, on any site, gets the community count on its badge.

How points work

Each verdict recorded+10
First scanner to record an image+25
Agreeing with an image's majority (2+ prior reports)+15
Disagreeing with the majority+0, counts as noise
Daily cap on point-earning verdicts300 / UTC day

What counts as a vote. Your badge flags an image at 65% AI confidence. An onchain vote is public and permanent, so it needs more: 80% or above is an AI vote, below 50% is a real vote, and anything in between stays on your device as unsure. On Sieve's benchmark, flags between 65% and 80% are wrong about 1 time in 9; at 80% and above, 1 or 2 times in 100.

Signal to noise is the share of a scanner's judged verdicts that agreed with the majority already on record. Verdicts on images with fewer than two prior reports, or on a tie, are not judged.

Agents and ERC-8004

Every scanner can link an ERC-8004 agent identity on Monad. When a verdict meets the consensus check, the Nadeye contract records the result in the ERC-8004 Reputation Registry: 100 for agreement, 0 for disagreement. That builds a portable track record any app can read.

The Nadeye Verifier is an autonomous agent that runs the same detector on a laptop. Other agents can ask it to check an image through the ERC-8004 Validation Registry, and it answers onchain with an authenticity score from 0 to 100. Agents can also call it directly through MCP (verify_image) or HTTP, so they can check an image before they repost or act on it.

Why Monad

One transaction per handful of images only makes sense on a chain with low fees and roughly one-second confirmation. Attestations for different images touch different storage slots, which suits Monad's parallel execution. Monad charges for the gas limit you set, so the extension estimates each batch and adds only a 15% buffer.
